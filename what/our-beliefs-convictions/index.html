Our Beliefs & Convictions

Our Foundation

What We Believe

CrossTalk Global is built on a clear evangelical foundation.

We affirm the deity of Jesus Christ and the inerrancy of Scripture. We stand in agreement with the Lausanne Covenant — one of the most significant statements of evangelical faith and mission ever produced.

CrossTalk is a trans-denominational organization. We work across church traditions and denominations, united by our shared commitment to Scripture and the gospel of Jesus Christ.

Core Convictions

Four Convictions That Drive Everything

These four premises are not merely organizational values — they are biblical convictions that shape every decision CrossTalk makes.

  1. God communicates to people of every culture through the Bible

    Scripture is not culturally limited or ethnically specific. God's Word speaks to every people group, every language, every context — without exception.

  2. Ordinary people everywhere can learn to understand Scripture

    The Bible is not the exclusive property of academics or ordained clergy. With proper training, any person of faith can learn to understand and apply God's Word.

  3. People can be trained to share biblical content effectively within their communities

    Communication is a learnable skill. CrossTalk's training equips people to present biblical truths in ways that connect meaningfully within their own cultural setting.

  4. Equipped individuals can then train others in biblical understanding and communication

    The multiplication principle. Those who are trained become trainers themselves — creating an exponential expansion of biblical literacy across cultures.

The Lausanne Covenant

CrossTalk Global endorses the Lausanne Covenant, a landmark 1974 statement that brought together evangelical leaders from more than 150 nations to affirm shared biblical commitments to the gospel and world mission.

This commitment reflects our deep concern for both the truth of the gospel and its proclamation to every people group on earth — what the Lausanne movement calls the "whole church taking the whole gospel to the whole world."
