Why CrossTalk?

Four Foundational Reasons

Why Does CrossTalk Global Exist?

CrossTalk Global was founded on four foundational convictions about what people need and how God works in the world.

Reason 1

People Need the Bible

The Bible is essential for human flourishing and salvation. It is God's word to humanity — not simply a collection of ancient writings, but the living voice of God speaking to every generation and every culture.

Scripture is not just one resource among many. It is the foundation upon which lives, communities, and churches are built. Without it, people are left without the direction, hope, and truth they need.

"For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life." — John 3:16
Reason 2

People Need Help Understanding the Bible

Bible ownership alone is not enough. The story of Philip and the Ethiopian eunuch (Acts 8) illustrates this perfectly — though the Ethiopian had access to Scripture, he needed someone to help him understand it.

Paul's rhetorical questions in Romans 10:13–15 underscore the same truth: "How can they hear without someone preaching to them? And how can anyone preach unless they are sent?"

People need trained, equipped biblical communicators who can unlock the Word of God within their cultural context.

Reason 3

God Communicates Person-to-Person

Throughout history, God has consistently worked through human intermediaries to convey His message. Moses, Jesus, Timothy — God does not shout at us from heaven.

He speaks through people. He has always chosen to work through human messengers, and He continues to do so today. This is why equipping biblical communicators is not simply a good idea — it is God's own method.

The call to train trainers is not a strategy CrossTalk invented. It is the pattern established in Scripture itself, from Moses training Joshua to Paul equipping Timothy to teach others.

Reason 4

The Global Need Is Enormous

Approximately 4,000 churches start every week worldwide — with projections suggesting 12,000 per week within three years. Yet the vast majority of these congregations are led by people with little or no formal biblical training.

"Only 5% of all pastoral leaders presently in ministry have received training; 2 million pastors lack necessary training."

Traditional seminaries have geographic, educational, and financial barriers that restrict access for most of the world's Christian leaders. CrossTalk exists to break down those barriers.
